About me

My current research builds agentic large language model systems for scientific discovery and validation, alongside physics-informed and interpretable machine learning for gravitational-wave astrophysics. I work on compact object mergers, from detection and population inference through to modeling their progenitors. More broadly, my interests span astrophysics, gravitational-wave physics, cosmology and astroparticle physics, together with applications of AI and machine learning across these domains; earlier work covered cosmological pipelines for spectroscopic surveys, gas physics for CMB-SZ experiments, and dark matter phenomenology in dwarf galaxies. Check out my research page for more info.

I'm an assistant professor in the physics department at UT Austin, and co-chair of the NASA AI/ML Science and Technology Interest Group. I was previously a postdoctoral fellow at Johns Hopkins University (JHU), and before that a member at the Institute for Advanced Study (IAS). I completed my PhD at the Center for Cosmology and Particle Physics (CCPP) at New York University (NYU) in 2021, and also worked closely with the CCA cosmology group.

I grew up in Nanded in India and did my bachelors in Engineering Physics at IIT Bombay. If you don't find me at my office, I may be backpacking, running or practicing latin and ballroom dancing.
