About

I build AI agents that do scientific research.

I'm an assistant professor in the physics department at UT Austin, in the Weinberg Institute for Theoretical Physics and the Center for Gravitational Physics. My group develops agentic AI systems for scientific discovery, alongside research in gravitational-wave astrophysics and large-scale-structure cosmology. I also co-chair the NASA AI/ML Science and Technology Interest Group.

I was previously a postdoctoral fellow at Johns Hopkins University (JHU), and before that a member at the Institute for Advanced Study (IAS). I completed my PhD at the Center for Cosmology and Particle Physics (CCPP) at New York University in 2021, working closely with the CCA cosmology group, and did my bachelors in Engineering Physics at IIT Bombay.

I grew up in Nanded in India. If you don't find me at my office, I may be backpacking, running or practicing latin and ballroom dancing.

Research

Research directions

Agentic AI for scientific discovery

Building agentic large language model systems that carry out research tasks, and that check their own results. research.html has no section on this yet, so the heading links to the page as a whole.

Gravitational-wave astrophysics

Searching LIGO–Virgo data with waveforms that include higher-order harmonics rather than the quadrupole mode alone, which has turned up new black hole mergers with astrophysically interesting properties — and modelling their populations and progenitors.

Large-scale structure cosmology

Analytic covariance matrices for the galaxy power spectrum, more than four orders of magnitude faster than simulating thousands of mock catalogues, validated against a full-shape analysis of SDSS-BOSS.
